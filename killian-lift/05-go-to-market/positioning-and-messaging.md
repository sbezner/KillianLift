# Positioning and Messaging

## 1. Three Positioning Options

We can position the Killian Lift in three distinct ways. Each leads to different messaging, packaging, target customers, and competitive moats. The recommendation comes after each option is evaluated.

### Option A: "The Smart Lift" (recommended)

**Headline: The first material lift that knows what it's doing.**

Position against Genie and Sumner as the differentiated, software-enabled product. Lead with telemetry, load cell, tilt cutoff, and cloud dashboard. The drill-drive is just one of several reasons to upgrade.

- **Target buyer:** rental fleet manager (Sarah persona). HVAC service manager (Tom persona). Anyone who values data and uptime.
- **Messaging emphasis:** Safety, accountability, fleet management.
- **Competitive moat:** software, data, and the network effect of telemetry across the installed base.
- **Pros:** highest LTV, defensible against fast-followers (hardware can be copied; data and brand cannot), best fit for rental fleet conversion.
- **Cons:** longer sales cycle, requires customer education, may scare away the lone-wolf pipefitter who just wants to stop cranking.

### Option B: "The Cordless Lift"

**Headline: Stop cranking. Press the button.**

Position against the entire manual lift category. Lead with the brushless DC drive and cordless tool battery support. Telemetry is mentioned but not the headline.

- **Target buyer:** the operator (Mike persona).
- **Messaging emphasis:** Stop the physical pain. Use the batteries you already own.
- **Competitive moat:** first to market with native battery (JET drill-drive launched Oct 2025 but is bare-tool only).
- **Pros:** Easiest to explain, fastest to demo, biggest emotional hit. Wins on contractor forums and YouTube.
- **Cons:** JET will follow within 12 to 18 months. Less defensible long-term. Underemphasizes the cloud which is the long-term moat.

### Option C: "The Pipefitter's Lift"

**Headline: Built by pipefitters, for pipefitters.**

Position narrowly against the Sumner Roust-A-Bout. Lead with the 1,500 lb capacity, doorway clearance, and pipefitter-specific accessories (pipe cradle, Victaulic spool tray).

- **Target buyer:** pipefitter contractor, Mechanical contractor.
- **Messaging emphasis:** Trade-specific. Built by people who've actually used a Roust-A-Bout for 20 years.
- **Competitive moat:** brand resonance with the trade. Hard to displace if we own the pipefitter mindshare.
- **Pros:** sharp positioning, easy to win the segment.
- **Cons:** small TAM. Pipefitters are ~10% of the material lift market. Limits growth.

### Recommendation: Option A (The Smart Lift)

Reasoning:

1. The TAM is largest under Option A (all material lift buyers, not just pipefitters).
2. The competitive moat is most defensible (software, data, network effect).
3. Phase 3 economics (rental fleet conversion) require Option A messaging.
4. We can use Option B's messaging tactically in operator-targeted channels (YouTube install video, contractor forums) without abandoning the Smart Lift positioning at the top of the brand.
5. Option C's messaging is the right voice for a single segment (Mike persona) but cannot be the brand.

The brand voice is **The Smart Lift**. The execution-level marketing tactically borrows Option B's "stop cranking" line for operator channels and Option C's "built for the trade" line for trade-specific outreach.

## 2. Brand Voice and Tone

**Direct, confident, no hedging.** No marketing-speak. No "designed to" or "engineered for."

Examples:

- Bad: "The Killian Smart Kit is engineered to provide an enhanced operator experience while reducing fatigue."
- Good: "The Killian Smart Kit replaces your hand crank with a battery and a button. Lifts 2x faster. Tells you the load weight."

- Bad: "Our advanced telemetry platform leverages cloud connectivity to deliver actionable insights."
- Good: "Every lift on your fleet shows up on a single dashboard. You see who's using it, where it is, and when the cable is due for inspection."

- Bad: "Safety-first design with multiple redundant systems."
- Good: "Load cell, tilt sensor, and E-stop. The lift refuses to lift more than 105% of rated. It refuses to extend above 10 ft on a 1.6 deg slope. It's harder to use this unsafely than to use it safely."

The tone is engineer-to-engineer. The buyer is a contractor or fleet manager who knows their job. They don't need to be sold on safety. They need to know what's in the box and what it does.

## 3. Tagline and Pitch Variants

### Primary tagline

**Killian Lift. The Smart Lift.**

(Two words, immediate, owns the category.)

### Elevator pitch (30 sec, for general audience)

The construction material lift is a 1980s product. We rebuilt it. The Killian Lift uses your cordless tool batteries, weighs 30% less, lifts twice as fast, and reports its location, load, and condition to a phone app. We sell a $700 retrofit kit for existing Sumner Roust-A-Bouts shipping in Q3 2026, and a clean-sheet $3,500 lift in Q2 2027.

### Operator pitch (15 sec, for contractor)

Stop cranking. Drop in a battery, press the button. 50 seconds to 25 ft with a 1,500 lb load. Audible alarm at 90% capacity, hard stop at 105%. 30 minute install on your existing Roust-A-Bout. $699.

### Fleet pitch (45 sec, for rental manager)

Every lift in your fleet on a single dashboard. Live location, live cycle count, live load. GPS theft recovery. Geofence alerts. Predictive cable replacement. Daily inspection log automatically completed. Your customer pays a 15% premium because the lift is actually the right tool, not a 1980s relic. We have a 50 unit free pilot ready to ship.
